# Chapter 15: Appendix

## Formula Reference

The representative formulas used in this document are summarized below.

### Weight Normalization

Weights $w_j$ are handled so that they sum to 1.

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{n} w_j = 1
$$

### Pareto Dominance

For a minimization problem, the condition under which individual $a$ dominates individual $b$ is that $a$ is at least as good on every objective and strictly better on at least one objective.

$$
f_j(a) \le f_j(b) \quad \forall j
$$

$$
\exists k \quad f_k(a) < f_k(b)
$$

### Min-Max Normalization

Benefit-type criterion:

$$
r_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - x_j^{\min}}{x_j^{\max} - x_j^{\min}}
$$

Cost-type criterion:

$$
r_{ij} = \frac{x_j^{\max} - x_{ij}}{x_j^{\max} - x_j^{\min}}
$$

### Vector Normalization

$$
r_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m} x_{ij}^2}}
$$

### Weighted Sum Score

$$
S_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} w_j r_{ij}
$$

### TOPSIS Score

$$
D_i^+ = \sqrt{\sum_j (v_{ij} - A_j^+)^2}
$$

$$
D_i^- = \sqrt{\sum_j (v_{ij} - A_j^-)^2}
$$

$$
\mathrm{score}_i = \frac{D_i^-}{D_i^+ + D_i^-}
$$

### VIKOR Compromise Index

$$
Q_i = v \cdot \frac{S_i - S^*}{S^- - S^*} + (1 - v) \cdot \frac{R_i - R^*}{R^- - R^*}
$$

### PROMETHEE Net Flow

$$
\Phi^{+}(i) = \frac{1}{m-1} \sum_{b \neq i} \pi(i, b)
$$

$$
\Phi^{-}(i) = \frac{1}{m-1} \sum_{b \neq i} \pi(b, i)
$$

$$
\Phi^{net}(i) = \Phi^{+}(i) - \Phi^{-}(i)
$$

## Glossary

| Term | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| MCDM | Multi-Criteria Decision Making. A collective term for decision-making methods based on multiple evaluation criteria. |
| Alternative | A candidate subject to comparison. In multi-objective optimization, this refers to individuals, trials, and candidate solutions. |
| Individual | A single candidate solution generated by an optimization algorithm. |
| Pareto front | The set of non-dominated solutions that are not dominated by any other individual. |
| Pareto dominance | A relationship in which one individual is at least as good as another on every objective and strictly better on at least one objective. |
| Evaluation criterion | An aspect used to evaluate alternatives, such as cost, performance, or risk. |
| Decision matrix | A matrix that arranges the evaluation values of alternatives and evaluation criteria. |
| Weight | The relative importance of an evaluation criterion. |
| Benefit-type criterion | An evaluation criterion where larger values are more desirable. |
| Cost-type criterion | An evaluation criterion where smaller values are more desirable. |
| Normalization | The process of converting values with different units or scales into a comparable form. |
| Positive ideal solution | A hypothetical solution composed of the most desirable value for each evaluation criterion. |
| Negative ideal solution | A hypothetical solution composed of the least desirable value for each evaluation criterion. |
| Sensitivity analysis | An analysis that checks the impact on results when weights or input values are varied. |
| Outranking | A concept of comparing alternatives by their dominance relations. |

## Method Selection Guide

The following is a guide for selecting an MCDM method.

| Purpose | Recommended Method | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simply produce an overall score first | WSM | Easy to implement and explain. |
| Select candidates close to the ideal solution | TOPSIS | Easy to explain in terms of distance from the ideal. |
| Select a compromise solution | VIKOR | Can balance overall utility and maximum regret. |
| Organize the rationale for weights | AHP | Enables pairwise comparisons and consistency checking. |
| Examine preference relations in detail | PROMETHEE | Can analyze wins and losses between alternatives. |
| Select or exclude candidates | ELECTRE | Well suited to narrowing based on dominance relations. |

For initial analysis, starting with easily explainable methods such as WSM or TOPSIS and then adding confirmation with VIKOR or PROMETHEE as needed is the most practical approach.

When selecting a method, prioritize the ability of decision makers to understand the results and explain the preconditions over computational sophistication.
